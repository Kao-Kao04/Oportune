## Charges that stuck

1. Features keep getting built (four on 2026-10-25) while the app has never gone live, against the team's own "Don't build more features" rule. Eight jurors relied on this.
2. Nobody has shown demand yet: there is one user (the founder), zero interviews and no organizer asked to pay. Six jurors relied on this.
3. The promise doesn't match the content: 52 of 79 listings have no fixed date and 63 of 79 sources are "other", so "never miss a deadline for verified opportunities" fails on the first screen. Five jurors relied on this.

## What the defense got right

1. The app is nearly ready to test. What remains is setup work (keys, domain, email, admin account), not a rebuild, and the team wrote its own kill rule in advance.
2. The beachhead has a visible gap. The research found no dedicated Philippine hackathon directory and no PH-organized events in CTFtime's 2026 list.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. Freeze features. Rotate the leaked keys, create the admin account, and deploy a live link.
2. Fix the first screen. Date or recurrence-tag the hackathon/CTF/OJT listings and favour official sources, so the tagline holds.
3. Put the app in front of the two campus IT orgs on a dated test: record student interviews, set minimum counts for interviews and pilot sign-ups, and quote at least one organizer a sponsored-listing price.

## The sentence

The court finds the building sound but the plan out of order. Commit no more feature code. Spend this week switching the app on, making its first screen honest, and booking the two orgs and your first interviews. Then let the team's own kill rule decide whether the project continues.
